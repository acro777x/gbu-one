## Part 1. What the Gem page does (from your screenshot)

| Section | What it does | Keep or change |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Big serif headline left, one phone right sitting on a coloured circle, one Download button | Keep the calm hero and the single CTA. Drop the circle and floating dots. |
| Gradient quote band | Press quote (Wired) on a blue to purple gradient | Change. GBU One has no press. Never invent a quote. Use a real fact band instead (see below). |

| Alternating feature rows | Phone on one side, text on the other, numbered 01 / 02 / 03 | Keep the alternating rhythm. Drop the numbering (the features are not a sequence). |
| Stock photography | Women's portraits that say nothing about the app | Drop. Use real app screens and the 19 avatars. |
| Decorative shapes | Triangle, X, squiggle, dot | Drop. They carry no information. |
| Closing CTA band | Gradient with an arrow and a second Download button | Keep the idea of a closing CTA, make it plain and solid. |
| Dark footer | Links, store badge | Keep, add install help and disclaimer. |

What makes it feel good: generous whitespace, one idea per section, a calm headline, product shown early.
What makes it feel dated or generic: stock photos, decoration without meaning, vague copy ("mindless feeds"), gradients as the main visual idea.

## Part 2. What the research says about app landing pages

Sources reviewed: Landingi (20 app landing pages, June 2026), Bitly app landing page guide (2026), Unicorn Platform mobile app template guide, KlientBoost app landing page teardown, Webstacks mobile landing page article (July 2026), Wishpond mobile examples.

Findings that apply here:

1. The hero must say what the app is and who it is for within the first screen on a phone. Most visitors arrive on a phone.
2. Keep to two or three calls to action. Repeating the same one is fine, offering five different ones is not.
3. Show the real app early. Clean visuals plus screenshots plus short copy beats long explanations.
4. Mobile layouts should be scannable: short blocks, big tap targets, fast load. Heavy pages lose people on campus Wi-Fi and mobile data.
5. Trust signals matter. For GBU One the honest ones are: no password asked, works offline, free, what the APK checksum is, who built it.
6. Privacy-first wording on buttons builds trust ("Download", not "Get started now!!!").
7. Using the same brand codes in the page and the app makes the product feel real (KlientBoost's SLOWLY example). So the page uses GBU One's own colours, fonts, avatars and card style.

## Part 3. How this page beats Gem

1. It is specific. Gem sells an idea. GBU One answers "where is my 9:30 class" and "where is last year's paper".
2. One memorable interactive moment: pick your school and the phone and avatar recolour to that school. It is the product's own personality, not decoration.
3. Real numbers from the app only (841+ subjects, 8 schools, 19 avatars, alarm 10 minutes before class, +50 points per approved paper).
4. Honest install path with checksum and steps. Most student apps skip this and lose trust.
5. Works as a phone page first, with a sticky download bar.

## Part 4. Things that make a page feel AI-made (and what we do instead)

| Tell | Instead |
|---|---|
| Every section is a row of three identical rounded cards | Mix: one phone with text, one full-width list, one podium, one table |
| Gradient band as decoration | Solid navy band that carries real numbers |
| Tiny ALL-CAPS label above every heading | No eyebrows. Headings stand alone. |
| One word in a headline in a different colour | Plain headlines, one colour |
| 01 / 02 / 03 on things that are not steps | Numbers only on the install steps |
| Fade-up animation on every section | One moment of motion (the school picker), everything else still |
| Stock people photos | Real screenshots and the avatars |
| Buzzwords ("seamless", "empower", "revolutionise") | Plain verbs, sentence case |
| Fake testimonials and user counts | None. Use only facts from `07_APP_FACTS.md`. |
